Research

My dissertation builds a replicable, national-data framework for evaluating Medicaid Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers that serve people with intellectual and developmental disabilities (IDD), with a focus on the Direct Support Professional workforce. North Carolina's Innovations Waiver is the proof-of-concept case.

Dissertation

Medicaid HCBS waivers and the IDD workforce

Economic impact modeling with IMPLAN (statewide, urban and rural) and difference-in-differences designs to estimate effects on service use, spending and access.

Program evaluation

Cost savings of High-Fidelity Wraparound

A Medicaid claims-based evaluation of wraparound services for youth in North Carolina, and what tiered care coordination means for equitable access.

Fair lending

Disparities in mortgage approvals

Regression models on HMDA data estimating how loan approval probabilities differ by applicant race and gender. Code on GitHub.

Digital health

Reports & white papers

  1. 2023
    Disparate access to credit: investigating North Carolina's residential mortgage market, 2018–2021
    Buaka, E. S. & Sills, S. J.
    White paper, Research, Policy, and Impact Center (RPIC), National Institute of Minority Economic Development
    Abstract

    Using Home Mortgage Disclosure Act (HMDA) records for North Carolina from 2018 to 2021, this white paper asks whether access to mortgage credit differs by applicant race, ethnicity and sex. We describe loan, applicant and community characteristics statewide and in seven metro areas (Raleigh, Durham, Greensboro/High Point, Winston-Salem, Charlotte, Asheville and Wilmington), then estimate logistic regression models of loan approval that hold constant loan type, purpose, occupancy, community characteristics and the loan-to-income ratio. Hispanic, Black and Asian applicants had significantly lower odds of approval than non-Hispanic White applicants: compared with White men, the odds were roughly 41–44% lower for Hispanic applicants, 35–38% lower for Black applicants and 29–32% lower for Asian applicants, while White women had slightly higher odds. Government-backed loans, refinancing, home-improvement loans and manufactured homes were also linked to lower approval, and community characteristics mattered less than expected and varied by city. Because HMDA does not record credit history or debt-to-income ratios, these are associations rather than proof of discrimination, but they point to structural barriers in the state's mortgage market that warrant further fair-lending scrutiny.

  2. 2025
    Economic impact of NC OSHERC graduates: a preliminary IMPLAN analysis
    Nienow, S. & Buaka, E. S.
    Memo for RTI International and the North Carolina Occupational Safety and Health Education and Research Center, UNC Gillings School of Global Public Health
    Abstract

    The North Carolina Occupational Safety and Health Education and Research Center (NC OSHERC) trains graduate-level professionals who protect worker health. This analysis characterizes the occupations and industries that employ occupational safety and health specialists and estimates the economic contribution of the center's graduates. Using IMPLAN input-output modeling (2023 data, 2025 dollars), we distributed graduates across 12 industries based on national employment patterns, assumed 70% remain working in North Carolina, and estimated direct, indirect and induced effects on jobs, labor income, value added, output and tax revenue for a single graduating cohort, for all graduates from 2018 to 2023, and for the U.S. economy as a whole. Each graduate employed in the state supports about one additional job elsewhere in the economy, and the 2018–2023 graduates together support an estimated 236 jobs and $65.7 million in economic output in North Carolina. We close with gaps in current knowledge and priorities for future research, including graduate surveys and the value of continuing education.

  3. 2026
    Alternative schools and student re-entry: evidence from the Newcomers Program and SCALE alternative schools
    Buaka, E. S. D.
    White paper prepared for Guilford County Schools
    Abstract

    Guilford County Schools runs two transitional alternative programs: the Newcomers Program, for recently arrived English Learner students, and SCALE, for students placed after serious disciplinary incidents. Using linked, de-identified student records from 2022–23 through 2024–25, this white paper offers the first longitudinal look at both programs: who they serve, how students do during placement, and what happens after they return to traditional schools. The analysis combines comparisons with English Learner peers, within-student before-during-after comparisons, and a survival analysis of time to disciplinary re-incident. Both programs show gains during enrollment. Newcomers students are more likely than comparable peers to reach the GPA needed for dual enrollment, and SCALE students' out-of-school suspensions drop sharply. For both groups, though, the first year back in a traditional school is the highest-risk period. The paper recommends structured first-year re-entry support that starts automatically when students transition.

Course papers, Central Michigan University

  1. 2021
    An industrial organization analysis of Netflix in the online television market
    Buaka, E. S. D.
    Term paper, ECO 425, Central Michigan University
    Abstract

    This paper examines Netflix from an industrial organization perspective, looking at how market structure, market share, barriers to entry, product differentiation and price elasticity of demand shape its strategy in the online television market. It traces Netflix's move from mail-order DVD rentals to streaming and original programming, and argues that its ad-free, on-demand original content has kept subscriber demand relatively inelastic despite price increases. Because competitors such as Hulu and Amazon Prime now produce their own originals, continued investment in original content is central to Netflix keeping its lead.

  2. 2020
    Effect of educational attainment on income
    Buaka, E. S. D.
    Term paper, ECO 586, Central Michigan University
    Abstract

    Using cross-sectional data from the 2005 Canadian Community Health Survey, this paper estimates the relationship between educational attainment and income, with age and sex as additional explanatory variables. The regression results support the hypothesis that education is a key determinant of income. Because work experience, education quality and other relevant factors are not observed in the data, the estimates are likely affected by omitted variable bias.
